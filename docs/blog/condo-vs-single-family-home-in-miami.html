Condo vs. Single-Family Home in Miami: Which Is Right for You?

By Vinicius Cadengue, Compass

This is one of the first questions I ask every buyer, because it shapes almost everything else about the search — budget, neighborhoods, timeline, and lifestyle. Here's how I'd frame the tradeoffs.

Upfront Cost vs. Ongoing Cost

Condos are often more accessible on price per square foot, especially in buildings further from the water, but HOA fees add a real monthly cost on top of the mortgage — sometimes substantial in older or amenity-heavy buildings. Single-family homes have no HOA in most cases, but you're responsible for every repair yourself, and insurance tends to run higher.

Insurance Is a Bigger Factor Than It Used to Be

In South Florida specifically, property insurance costs vary a lot by building age, roof age, and flood zone. For condos, check the building's master insurance policy and reserve fund health before you fall in love with a unit — a special assessment can be an expensive surprise. For single-family homes, get an insurance quote during your due-diligence period, not after closing.

Space, Privacy, and Maintenance

Single-family homes generally win on space, privacy, and the ability to renovate freely. Condos win on low-maintenance living, building amenities (gym, pool, security), and often a more central location relative to price. Neither is objectively "better" — it depends on how hands-on you want to be as an owner.

Resale Considerations

Both sell well in Miami's market when priced and marketed correctly, but buyer pools differ — single-family homes tend to draw more owner-occupant buyers, while condos see a mix of owner-occupants and investors. I factor this into pricing strategy whichever direction you're buying or selling.
