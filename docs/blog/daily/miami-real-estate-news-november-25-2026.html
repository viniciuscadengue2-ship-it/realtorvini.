Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 25, 2026: Miami Home Prices Rise 4.6%, Third-Best in the U.S., as Mortgage Rates Slip to 7.14%

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 25, 2026

Good morning, Miami. On the day before Thanksgiving, the national numbers are saying something good about our market: Miami home prices are growing faster than almost anywhere else in the country. Mortgage rates also gave back some of Monday's jump, a 55-story rental tower is moving forward in Edgewater, and Little Haiti broke ground on a new kind of affordable homeownership. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Home Prices

1. Miami climbs into Case-Shiller's top three for price growth

Miami-area home prices were up 4.6% in September compared with a year earlier, according to the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller indices released on November 24. That puts Miami third among the 20 big metro areas the index tracks, behind Chicago at 6.9% and New York at 5.8%. Miami had been in fourth place in July and August. "Miami's rise is particularly notable," said Rebecca Kaufman of S&P Dow Jones Indices.

Nationally, prices rose 2.1% over the year, the same pace as in August, while the 20-city composite gained 2.6% and the 10-city composite 3.5%, HousingWire reported. Seattle had the weakest result at about minus 1.9%, followed by Denver and Dallas, and Tampa was slightly negative. With inflation at 3.7% in September, S&P said national home prices fell in real terms for the 16th month in a row.

My take: This is the number I'd show anyone who thinks Miami prices are falling apart. Sales volume has slowed, and condos in older buildings are a different story, but well-located single-family homes are still gaining value faster than most of the country. Keep in mind that Case-Shiller is a three-month moving average of repeat sales, so it describes the summer, not this week. If you're selling a house in a good school zone or a dry, renovated property, price it on recent comparable sales, not on gloomy headlines. If you're buying, waiting for a big price drop in those segments has not paid off so far.

Source: S&P Cotality Case-Shiller release, Nov. 24 (PR Newswire) · HousingWire, Nov. 24 · South Florida Agent Magazine, Nov. 24

Mortgage Rates

2. Rates ease to 7.14% heading into a busy Wednesday

The national average 30-year fixed rate was 7.14% on Tuesday, November 24, according to Zillow data published by Yahoo Finance, down 4 basis points from the day before and 25 basis points from a week earlier. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.50%. Mortgage Research Center data reported by JournalArta showed a similar drop, to 7.145% for the 30-year. Freddie Mac's latest weekly average, from November 19, remains 7.28%, as listed by Mortgage Daily.

Today is packed with data before the holiday: the October PCE inflation report, the second estimate of third-quarter GDP and new home sales, according to Mortgage Daily, which also expects Freddie Mac to publish this week's survey today, as it has on the Wednesday of Thanksgiving week in each of the past two years. Bond markets are closed Thursday and close early Friday. The Federal Reserve's next rate decision is on December 9, according to both Mortgage Daily and JournalArta.

My take: A quarter-point drop in a week is worth about $85 a month in principal and interest on a $500,000 loan (7.14% versus 7.39%). That's not life-changing, but it adds up over years. If you're under contract, today's inflation report is the kind of release that can move rates either way, so have your lock plan agreed with your lender before 8:30 a.m. If you're still shopping, remember that rates can be refinanced later; the house and the price you negotiate can't.

Source: Yahoo Finance (Zillow data), Nov. 24 · Mortgage Daily, Nov. 24 · JournalArta, Nov. 24

Edgewater

3. A 55-story, 285-unit tower clears a key approval near Margaret Pace Park

SB Development and Hazelton Capital Group have secured a major municipal approval for a 55-story residential tower at 419 NE 19th Street in Edgewater, YieldPro reported on November 20. The building, designed by Fogarty Finger, is planned with 285 residences overlooking Biscayne Bay and Margaret Pace Park. The partners bought the site in 2022.

The project joins a busy stretch of the Edgewater waterfront, where The Cove Residences is already under construction nearby. YieldPro also noted that the Omni Community Redevelopment Agency approved up to $10 million in September for the first phase of improvements to Margaret Pace Park.

My take: Edgewater keeps adding supply, which is good news for renters and for buyers who want a newer building near the bay. For owners in older Edgewater condos, it means more competition when you sell or rent, so the units that stand out will be the ones in buildings with their milestone inspections and reserves in order. If you're comparing pre-construction with resale in this neighborhood, I can pull the numbers for both side by side.

Source: YieldPro, Nov. 20

Little Haiti

4. Little Haiti breaks ground on Miami-Dade's first community land trust homes

The South Florida Community Land Trust has broken ground on Place Louverture, 13 permanently affordable one-, two- and three-bedroom flats and townhomes at 6601 NE Miami Place in Little Haiti, Miami Living reported on November 13. It is the first community land trust homeownership project in Miami-Dade County. Buyers will own their homes while the nonprofit keeps the land under a renewable 99-year ground lease, which lowers the purchase price.

The homes are aimed at households earning 60% to 120% of the area median income. Funders named include the City of Miami, Miami-Dade County, Citibank, JPMorganChase and the Little Haiti Revitalization Trust, and Mayor Daniella Levine Cava and Commissioner Christine King attended the groundbreaking, according to Miami Living and LatiNation. "Place Louverture represents so much more than 13 new homes," said Mandy Bartle, the land trust's CEO.

My take: Thirteen homes won't change the market, but the model matters. Land trust homes come with resale rules that keep them affordable for the next buyer, so they suit people who want stability and equity more than a big profit. If you're a teacher, nurse or first responder priced out of Miami, ask me about land trust and down payment assistance programs before you assume ownership is out of reach.

Source: Miami Living, Nov. 13 · LatiNation, Nov. 16

Miami real estate questions people are asking

Are Miami home prices still going up?

Yes. The S&P Cotality Case-Shiller index released November 24, 2026 shows Miami-area home prices up 4.6% year over year in September, the third-fastest growth among the 20 metro areas it tracks, behind Chicago and New York.

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

The national average 30-year fixed rate was 7.14% on November 24, 2026, according to Zillow data, and Freddie Mac's latest weekly average was 7.28% on November 19. Your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

When is the next Federal Reserve decision?

The Fed's next rate decision is scheduled for December 9, 2026. Mortgage rates often move before and after Fed meetings and major inflation reports.

What is a community land trust home?

You buy and own the house, but a nonprofit owns the land and leases it to you, usually for 99 years. That lowers the price, and resale rules keep the home affordable for the next buyer. Place Louverture in Little Haiti is the first such homeownership project in Miami-Dade.

Bottom line

Miami ranks third in the country for home price growth, and rates eased to 7.14% before today's inflation report. If you're buying, the holiday weeks bring fewer competitors; if you're selling a well-located home, the national data is on your side.
