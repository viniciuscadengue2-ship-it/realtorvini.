Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 20, 2026: Mortgage Rates Rise to 7.28% After Four Weekly Declines as Florida House Sales Fall 10.1%

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 20, 2026

Good morning, Miami. Mortgage rates turned higher this week after four straight declines, and the statewide October numbers show a Florida market that is selling less but still holding prices for houses. I also look at a boutique condo plan on Ocean Drive and why Brazil keeps showing up in Miami's investment pitch. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. The 30-year rate rises to 7.28%, its first weekly increase in five weeks

Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.28% on November 19, up from 7.11% the week before, ending four consecutive weekly declines. The 15-year average rose to 6.66% from 6.45%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.26% and the 15-year 5.54%.

Freddie Mac's chief economist, Sam Khater, pointed buyers to rate shopping, saying comparing quotes can "potentially save them thousands" over the life of a loan. The survey covers conventional purchase loans for borrowers with 20% down and excellent credit, so many real-world quotes will differ.

My take: On a $500,000 30-year loan, principal and interest is about $3,421 a month at 7.28%, roughly $58 more than at last week's 7.11% ($3,364) and about $339 more than at last year's 6.26% ($3,082). Khater's advice is the practical one: get at least two or three written quotes on the same day before you lock. If you're buying a condo, where sellers have more inventory to compete with, ask for a credit toward a rate buydown. Cash and foreign national buyers: higher rates keep some local buyers on the sidelines, which helps your negotiating position.

Source: Freddie Mac release (GlobeNewswire) · Freddie Mac investor relations · WRE News

Florida Market

2. Florida house sales fell 10.1% in October, the third monthly drop, while prices kept rising

Closed sales of existing single-family homes in Florida fell 10.1% in October from a year earlier, to 19,049, and condo and townhouse sales fell 5% to 6,862, Florida Realtors reported on November 16. It was the third straight month of year-over-year declines for both. New pending sales, a signal of closings to come, fell 15.7% for houses and 11.9% for condos and townhouses.

Prices split by property type. The statewide single-family median rose 2.2% to $420,000, its eighth straight yearly increase, while the condo and townhouse median fell 3.3% to $290,000. Active listings were down 7.4% for houses and 8.8% for condos and townhouses, leaving 4.6 and 8 months of supply. In a separate November 17 analysis, MIAMI Realtors chief economist Gay Cororaton said South Florida sales are likely to stay subdued through year-end.

My take: Miami is following the same pattern as the rest of Florida: fewer sales, but house prices holding because there still aren't many houses for sale. The drop in pending sales tells me the next couple of months will be slow too. If you're selling a house, price it right from day one and you're still in a good spot. If you're buying, especially a condo, slower months are when sellers are most open to credits and repairs. Want the October numbers for your zip code? Send me a WhatsApp and I'll pull them.

Source: Florida Realtors, October 2026 release · Florida Realtors news · MIAMI Realtors, South Florida market analysis

Miami Beach

3. Key International plans 27 luxury condos in place of the Marriott Stanton on Ocean Drive

Key International, led by the Ardid family, wants to replace the 224-room Marriott Stanton South Beach at 161 Ocean Drive with a 13-story condo building of about 295,353 square feet and just 27 residences, Commercial Observer reported on November 17. The 1.5-acre site is in South of Fifth, and the design is by Seattle firm Olson Kundig.

The plan keeps and restores the historic Ocean Drive facade of the hotel, which dates to 1939, while the Second Street side would be partly rebuilt. Key International has owned the hotel since 1997, and the project goes to Miami Beach's Historic Preservation Board on December 15.

My take: Twenty-seven homes on an oceanfront acre and a half tells you exactly who this is for: buyers who want space and privacy, not hotel-style units. If you own in South of Fifth, scarce new product like this supports values around you. If you're thinking about preconstruction, remember that a preservation board hearing is an early step; I wait for approvals, financing and a construction start before treating a project as real.

Source: Commercial Observer

International Buyers

4. Brazil ranks second as Miami-Dade's Beacon Council courts more foreign investment

Miami-Dade's economic development agency, the Beacon Council, is stepping up its outreach to Spain, Brazil and other European and Latin American markets, Miami Today reported on November 18. Spain is the council's leading international market, with more than 500 Spanish companies in Miami, and Brazil ranks second in both number of companies and investment volume.

Its president and CEO, Rodrick Miller, estimated 10 to 12 international trips or incoming delegations between October and the next two or three months, and said the top international priority is securing more foreign direct investment.

My take: When Brazilian companies open offices in Miami, their executives and families need places to live, and that is a big part of the demand I see in Brickell, Coconut Grove, Doral and Aventura. If you're a Brazilian buyer, plan the structure before you choose the property: how you'll hold title, how you'll move funds and whether you'll finance as a foreign national. Talk to a CPA or attorney who works on both sides, and I'll help with the rest, em português.

Miami real estate questions people are asking

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Freddie Mac's national weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.28% on November 19, 2026, up from 7.11% a week earlier, and 6.66% for a 15-year fixed. Miami buyers get rates close to national averages; your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

Are Florida home prices still going up?

For houses, yes. Florida Realtors reported that the statewide single-family median rose 2.2% to $420,000 in October 2026, while the condo and townhouse median fell 3.3% to $290,000.

Are home sales slowing in Florida?

Yes. Single-family closed sales fell 10.1% and condo and townhouse sales fell 5% in October 2026 from a year earlier, the third straight monthly decline, and new pending sales fell 15.7% and 11.9%, according to Florida Realtors.

Is now a good time for Brazilians to buy in Miami?

It depends on your goals and how you'll pay. Higher mortgage rates mean less local competition for cash and foreign national buyers, especially in condos. Plan title, funds transfer and taxes with a CPA or attorney before choosing a property.

Bottom line

Rates moved back up to 7.28% this week, so shop at least two or three lenders before you lock. Florida sales are slowing, but house prices are holding because supply is still tight. If you're buying a condo, use the slower market to negotiate.
