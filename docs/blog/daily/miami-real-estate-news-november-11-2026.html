Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 11, 2026: Mortgage Rates Fall for a Third Straight Week as Miami Waits on October Sales Data

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 11, 2026

Good morning, Miami. A short, useful edition today: rates eased again, our latest county numbers show a tighter market for houses, and two rule changes that matter a lot if you're buying a condo. Below each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. The 30-year rate drops to 7.18%, its third weekly decline in a row

Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.18% on November 5, down from 7.24% the week before, with the 15-year at 6.57%. It was the third straight weekly drop, though rates are still well above the 6.22% average of a year ago.

The next Freddie Mac reading comes out tomorrow, Thursday. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate steady at its late-October meeting, and some forecasters expect the weekly average to stay near 7% through November.

My take: On a $500,000 30-year loan, the move from 7.24% to 7.18% lowers principal and interest by about $20 a month (from roughly $3,407 to $3,387). The bigger number: that same loan costs about $318 a month more than it would have at last year's 6.22%. If you're buying, get pre-approved now and negotiate seller credits toward a rate buydown instead of waiting for a perfect rate. Cash and foreign national buyers: higher rates mean less local competition for you.

Source: Freddie Mac PMMS · AP via WCAX · RISMedia

Miami-Dade Market

2. Miami-Dade inventory is down about 10% from last year, and October numbers land Monday

The latest full county data (September) shows active residential listings down about 10% from a year earlier, with pending sales also down about 10% and homes that did sell going under contract faster than last year. The all-property median sale price was $570,000, up 2% year over year.

MIAMI Realtors releases October sales data on Monday, November 16. That report will show whether the September dip in closed sales carried into the fall.

My take: Fewer listings plus faster sales means well-priced houses still move, especially in Kendall, South Miami and the Gables. Sellers: the market rewards the right price on day one, not a high price you chase down later. Buyers: if a home fits, be ready to act. I'll break down Monday's report here on the blog.

Source: Echo Fine Properties, Miami-Dade September 2026 update (MIAMI MLS data) · MIAMI Realtors release schedule

Condos

3. Condo financing got stricter: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac ended the 'quick review' path for established buildings

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac retired the Limited Review and Streamlined Review options for established condo projects, a change that took effect August 3, 2026. In practice, more buildings now go through a full review of their finances and condition before a conventional loan can close.

My take: Before you fall in love with a unit, ask your lender early whether the building is approved for conventional financing. I also review the milestone inspection, the structural integrity reserve study (SIRS), recent board minutes and any planned assessments with every condo buyer. A building that can't be financed limits who can buy from you later.

Source: Manhattan Miami condo market update (cites Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 and Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C)

Insurance

4. Citizens' new clearinghouse is pushing more condo associations to private insurers

Under Florida's 2026 law on Citizens Property Insurance (SB 1028), condo associations and other commercial-residential properties must first be checked against new clearinghouses of private and surplus lines carriers. Citizens will not issue or renew those policies when a private offer comes in within 15% of Citizens' price.

My take: This affects a building's insurance cost, and that flows straight into HOA fees. When we look at a condo, I'll ask who insures the building, when the policy renews and whether a change is coming. It's one of the most overlooked line items in a Miami condo budget.

Source: Florida Department of Financial Services, 2026 legislative summary

Luxury

5. Miami-Dade $10M+ house sales rose in the third quarter while condo sales at that level dipped

Closings of houses at $10 million or more in Miami-Dade rose to 38 in the third quarter from 29 a year earlier, while condo closings at that level slipped to 15 from 17, according to a Q3 report built on MIAMI MLS data.

My take: The top of the market keeps choosing land and privacy, meaning houses on the water and in places like Gables Estates and Miami Beach's islands. For my international clients, the signal is that global money still treats Miami real estate as a place to hold dollars, which supports values further down the price range too.

Source: Manhattan Miami, Miami Ultra-Luxury Market Report Q3 2026

Miami real estate questions people are asking

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Freddie Mac's national weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.18% as of November 5, 2026, and 6.57% for a 15-year fixed. Miami buyers get rates in line with national averages; your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type. The next weekly figure is released Thursday, November 12.

Is Miami a buyer's or seller's market right now?

It depends on the property. Houses are tight, with countywide listings down about 10% from a year ago and homes selling faster. Condos give buyers more room to negotiate, but financing and insurance rules make the building's paperwork more important than ever.

Can I get a conventional loan on any Miami condo?

No. The building has to meet Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac standards, and since August 3, 2026 more established buildings need a full review. Ask your lender about the building before you make an offer.

When is the next Miami-Dade home sales report?

MIAMI Realtors releases October 2026 sales data on Monday, November 16, 2026.

Bottom line

Rates are drifting down slowly, houses remain scarce, and condo deals now live or die on the building's finances, insurance and financing approval. If you're buying a condo, check the building first. If you're selling a house, low inventory is on your side as long as you price it right.
