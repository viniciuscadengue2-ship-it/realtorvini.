Pinecrest

1. Five Pinecrest houses closed for $14.1 million in the first half of November

Five single-family homes in the Village of Pinecrest closed for a combined $14,140,920 between November 1 and 15, according to MLS-reported sales compiled in the Pinecrest Tribune's November 19 brief. The top sale was 5790 SW 96th Street, a five-bedroom, seven-bath home of 6,079 square feet on a 37,461-square-foot lot, which closed November 2 for $4.6 million. The highest price per square foot, $839, was paid for 7885 SW 117th Street, a 1953 home that sold for $3.5 million on November 10.

The other closings were 6500 SW 127th Street at $2,840,920, 7331 SW 117th Terrace at $1.7 million and 12900 SW 82nd Place at $1.5 million. The five homes averaged about $749 per square foot, and four of them were built in the 1950s and 1960s. The brief said no condo or townhome sale in the village could be independently confirmed for the period.

My take: Pinecrest prices are about land and schools as much as the house itself. Four of these five homes were built between 1953 and 1963, on lots of about 15,000 square feet or more, and buyers paid $682 to $839 per square foot anyway. If you're buying, budget for updates, check the roof, plumbing and electrical age, and get a wind mitigation report before you price insurance. If you're selling an older Pinecrest home, the lot is your main asset, so price with recent comparable sales, not with the national headlines about a slow market. Ask me for the sales on your street and I'll send them.

Source: Pinecrest Tribune, Nov. 19

Miami Beach

2. Russell Galbut lines up three new Miami Beach Live Local sites, and a commissioner calls it a nonstarter

Developer Russell Galbut of Crescent Heights said at Bisnow's South Florida Multifamily Summit that he has replaced three withdrawn Miami Beach applications with three new sites, two on Alton Road and one on 71st Street, and plans to use the Live Local Act, Bisnow reported on November 17. The withdrawn plans included a 43-story, 557-foot tower at 1575 Alton Road with more than 160 units, 62 of them for households earning 120% of the area median income, plus 28-story buildings at 740 Alton Road and 880 71st Street. For 1575 Alton, Galbut wrote that if the site isn't sold he will cut the height to 225 feet.

Galbut said he would evaluate the concepts and submit plans later this year or in 2027. As of November 17, no plans had been filed with the city, Commissioner Alex Fernandez told Bisnow, and Fernandez called the intent a nonstarter. The city denied a separate Live Local proposal at the end of October over zoning conflicts and directed its attorney to look at legal options.

My take: The Live Local Act lets developers go taller and denser than local zoning allows if they include workforce housing, and Miami Beach is one of the cities pushing back hardest. If you own near Alton Road or 71st Street, follow these filings, because a tower next door can change your views, traffic and resale story. If you're buying in Miami Beach, ask me to check what's been proposed on nearby lots before you make an offer. Nothing here is approved, and these fights often take years.

Source: Bisnow, Nov. 17

New Development

3. The biggest condo construction loans of 2026, led by $817 million for Faena

Haute Residence's November 21 ranking of South Florida's largest condo loans of 2026 lists nine deals worth about $3.27 billion combined, by my count. In Miami-Dade, the list includes $817 million for Faena Residences Miami on the Miami River (October 28), $507 million for a planned 28-story, 106-residence tower at 1250 West Avenue in Miami Beach that was financed before sales began, and $463 million to refinance and finish the 170-residence South Tower at The St. Regis Residences in Sunny Isles Beach, with delivery expected by the end of 2028.

It also lists $410 million for THE WELL Coconut Grove (February 3), a $220 million predevelopment loan from Blackstone Real Estate Debt Strategies for 301 Ocean Drive in Key Biscayne (November), and $132.5 million from Bank OZK for Ponce Park, an 11-story, 58-residence building in Coral Gables expected in early 2028. Tyko Capital was the lender on four of the five largest loans. Haute Residence discloses that THE WELL is one of its partners.

My take: Big loans tell you lenders still believe in Miami luxury condos, and a funded project is much less risky for a pre-construction buyer than one still waiting for money. Before you put down a deposit, ask whether the construction loan has closed, where your deposit is held, and what happens if the delivery date slips. If you own a resale condo in Sunny Isles, Coconut Grove or Miami Beach, expect more new competition from 2028 on, so keep your building's reserves and inspections in order. I'm happy to compare a new project and a resale unit for you side by side.

Source: Haute Residence, Nov. 21

Condo Costs

4. A Hollywood condo owner faces foreclosure over about $18,000 in special assessments

Janor Farthing, a 68-year-old owner in the Carriage Hills community in Hollywood, owes her condo association nearly $18,000 in special assessments for renovations to her 50-year-old building, even after United Way of Broward gave her close to $10,000, CBS News Miami reported. She has received a letter from the association's attorney threatening foreclosure and is waiting for a judge to decide whether she gets more time to pay. Moneywise, in a November 14 follow-up, reported her age and said she lives on about $1,700 a month in Social Security and pays $2,300 a month in assessments.

Moneywise also noted that under Florida Statutes 718.116(3), unpaid condo assessments can carry interest of up to 18% a year when the declaration doesn't set a rate, and that associations generally send several notices, including a notice of intent to record a lien and a notice of intent to foreclose, before going to court. United Way's housing vice president told CBS the help came from a one-time $200,000 grant.

My take: When an older building needs major repairs, the bill lands on every owner. If you're buying a condo, ask for the reserve study, the milestone inspection, and any assessments approved or under discussion, and get them in writing before you sign. If you already own and an assessment is coming, talk to the board early about payment plans, and talk to a condo attorney if you get a lien notice; don't wait for the foreclosure letter. On $18,000, 18% a year is $3,240 in interest alone. If you're thinking of selling because of an assessment, call me first so we can look at how buyers will price it.

Source: CBS News Miami · Moneywise via AOL, Nov. 14

Miami real estate questions people are asking

How much do homes cost in Pinecrest, Florida?

Five Pinecrest single-family homes closed between November 1 and 15, 2026, for $1.5 million to $4.6 million, averaging about $749 per square foot, according to MLS data compiled by the Pinecrest Tribune.

What is the Live Local Act in Miami Beach?

Florida's Live Local Act lets developers build taller and denser than local zoning allows if they include workforce housing. Miami Beach has pushed back on several proposals, and none of Russell Galbut's new sites had been filed as of November 17, 2026.

Can a Florida condo association foreclose over unpaid special assessments?

Yes. Associations can record a lien and foreclose after sending required notices, and unpaid assessments can carry interest of up to 18% a year if the declaration sets no rate. Talk to a condo attorney early if you fall behind.

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Mortgage News Daily's index put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.33% on Friday, November 20, 2026, down from 7.51% on November 16. Your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

Bottom line

Pinecrest houses are still selling for $1.5 million to $4.6 million even in a slow national market, Miami Beach is digging in against new Live Local towers, and lenders keep funding big condo projects. If you own an older condo, plan for assessments before they arrive. Daily rates ended last week at 7.33%.
