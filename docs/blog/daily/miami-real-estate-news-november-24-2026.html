Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 24, 2026: Mortgage Rates Jump to 7.38% to Start Thanksgiving Week as Citizens' Dec. 4 Takeout Deadline Nears

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 24, 2026

Good morning, Miami. It's a short holiday week, but a few things are moving that matter if you own, buy or insure a home here. Mortgage rates bounced higher on Monday after last week's slide, the next Citizens takeout deadline is ten days away, the City of Miami and Miami-Dade County are fighting over who controls redevelopment money in Allapattah, and the county is looking for a developer for 601 acres in Homestead. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. Rates bounce back up to 7.38% as a holiday-shortened week begins

After falling every day last week, mortgage rates turned higher on Monday. The 30-year fixed rate rose to 7.38% APR on November 23, according to rates Zillow provides to NerdWallet, 14 basis points above the previous day and 4 basis points above a week earlier. NerdWallet tied the move to a rally in tech stocks, which pulled money out of Treasuries. Freddie Mac's most recent weekly average, released November 19, was 7.28%, a figure also listed by Mortgage News Daily and Mortgage Daily. Mortgage Daily reported Optimal Blue's rate-lock index at 7.21% for the 30-year and 6.70% for the 15-year on Friday, November 20.

The calendar is packed before the holiday. Mortgage Daily lists the October PCE inflation report, the second estimate of third-quarter GDP and new home sales for Wednesday, November 25. The bond market is closed on Thanksgiving, and Friday is a shortened session.

My take: At Freddie Mac's 7.28%, principal and interest on a $500,000 30-year loan is about $3,421 a month. A 0.14-point swing like Monday's moves that payment by roughly $48. That's real money, but it's noise compared with choosing the right house. If you're under contract and closing in December, talk to your lender before Wednesday's inflation report and agree on when you'll lock, so a single headline doesn't decide it for you. If you're still shopping, the quiet holiday weeks are a good time to make offers.

Source: NerdWallet, Nov. 23 · Mortgage Daily, week of Nov. 23 · Mortgage News Daily

Insurance

2. Got a Citizens takeout letter? Your choice is due December 4

Citizens Property Insurance's 2026 personal lines calendar lists December 4 as the policyholder choice deadline for the round of takeout offers mailed October 28, with policies moving to private carriers on December 15. Citizens' depopulation page lists eight companies in that round: American Integrity, Condo Owners' Reciprocal Exchange, Manatee, Mangrove, One Alliance (formerly Universal North America), Security First, Slide and Southern Oak.

If a homeowner does nothing by the deadline, the calendar notes say Citizens assigns the policy to a company that selected it, and to the one with the lowest estimated premium if more than one did. CubaHeadlines reported that choices can be registered on Citizens' online portal with the policy number and registration code from the letter, or through an agent, and that a transfer is final once completed. Citizens' rules also say a policy with a private offer within 20% of its Citizens renewal estimate may no longer be eligible to stay with Citizens.

My take: Don't let this letter sit in a pile over Thanksgiving. Compare the offer line by line with your current policy: the hurricane deductible, the roof coverage, whether it pays replacement cost or actual cash value, and the company's financial rating. A lower premium can hide a much bigger deductible. If you're buying a home that's insured with Citizens, ask the seller whether they've received a takeout offer, because it can change what you'll pay. If you need an independent agent who will walk you through it, send me a WhatsApp and I'll share the ones I trust.

Source: Citizens 2026 Personal Lines Depopulation Calendar · Citizens, Depopulation Resources · CubaHeadlines

Allapattah

3. Miami and Miami-Dade are headed to court over Allapattah's redevelopment money

The fight over a new community redevelopment agency, or CRA, in Allapattah has moved to the legal stage. A November 20 headline in The Real Deal says the City of Miami has sued the county over the agency. Miami commissioners voted unanimously on September 24 to authorize the city attorney to sue, seeking a judge's ruling that the CRA is valid, The American Developer reported. The city created the CRA in April after the county let a state-mandated review period pass without acting, which the city treats as consent under state law.

The district covers roughly 1,660 acres, and its plan was designed around tax-increment financing projected to generate $243 million through 2055 for infrastructure, affordable housing and economic development, CBS Miami and The American Developer reported. A September 11 county letter argued that money for Allapattah's growth would be better spent on other priorities, such as the countywide SMART Plan transit program, than locked inside a city-controlled CRA. In the meantime, the city is funding the agency's operations from its general fund.

My take: CRAs matter for property owners because they decide where public money for streets, drainage and housing goes in a neighborhood for decades. Allapattah sits next to Wynwood and the Health District and has drawn a lot of development interest, so the outcome of this case will shape what gets built and how fast. If you're buying there, focus on what's already funded and under way rather than promises. If you own and are thinking of selling, it's worth talking about timing before a ruling. Nothing has been decided yet.

Source: The Real Deal, Nov. 20 · The American Developer, Sept. 25 · CBS Miami

South Dade

4. Miami-Dade may hire a master developer for its Homestead Spaceport Territory

A Miami-Dade commission committee backed a resolution from Commissioner Kionne McGhee asking Mayor Daniella Levine Cava to speed up development of Spaceport Territory, most of the 601 acres the county received from the Air Force in 2004 near Homestead Air Reserve Base, Miami Today reported on November 18. The state Legislature designated the land Florida Spaceport Territory in 2024.

The resolution, supported by the Infrastructure, Innovation and Technology Committee, asks the mayor to work with Space Florida to compete for aerospace projects, identify the infrastructure the sites need, keep out uses that don't fit an aerospace focus, connect the effort to local job training, and solicit contracts that could include firms to market the county land and possibly a master developer. If this year's budget can't cover it, she would have four months to tell commissioners and recommend funding sources. A defense industry advocate told Miami Today that companies need certainty about infrastructure and timelines.

My take: Large employers change housing demand. South Dade, from Homestead to Florida City, is still one of the more affordable parts of the county for single-family homes, and a real aerospace hub would add jobs close by. This is an early step, though, with no developer, money or tenants announced, so don't buy on the promise alone. If you're looking in Homestead anyway, check flood zones, commute times and HOA rules, and buy for what the area offers today.

Source: Miami Today, Nov. 18 · Florida Trend, Nov. 23

Miami real estate questions people are asking

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

The 30-year fixed rate rose to 7.38% APR on Monday, November 23, 2026, according to Zillow data published by NerdWallet. Freddie Mac's weekly average was 7.28% on November 19. Your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

What happens if I ignore a Citizens takeout offer letter?

For the round with a December 4, 2026 deadline, Citizens says that if you don't register a choice, your policy goes to a company that selected it, or to the one with the lowest estimated premium if several did. Policies in that round move on December 15.

What is the Allapattah CRA?

It's a community redevelopment agency the City of Miami created in April 2026 for roughly 1,660 acres of Allapattah, with a plan built around $243 million in projected tax-increment revenue through 2055. Miami-Dade County has opposed it, and the dispute is headed to court.

Is Thanksgiving week a good time to buy a home in Miami?

Fewer buyers are out during the holidays, which can mean less competition and more flexible sellers. Rates can still move, so keep in touch with your lender, especially around economic reports.

Bottom line

Rates bounced to 7.38% on Monday after a week of declines, and Wednesday's inflation report could move them again before Thanksgiving. If you have a Citizens takeout letter, decide by December 4. Allapattah's redevelopment fight and Homestead's aerospace plans are worth watching, but neither changes prices today.
