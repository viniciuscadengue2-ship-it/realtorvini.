Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 16, 2026: Faena Breaks Ground on Twin 68-Story Miami River Towers as Daily Mortgage Rates Hit 7.5%

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 16, 2026

Good morning, Miami. This Monday edition starts on the Miami River, where one of the biggest condo projects in the city just broke ground, then looks at where daily mortgage rates closed last week, the next wave of Brickell towers, and a big apartment sale in southwest Miami-Dade. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

New Construction

1. Faena Residences Miami breaks ground on two 68-story towers on the Miami River, backed by an $817 million loan

Construction has started on Faena Residences Miami, two 68-story towers of roughly 800 feet at 24 Southwest 4th Street on the Miami River in downtown Miami, Florida YIMBY reported on November 13. The project, from Fortune International Group and KAR Properties with Faena, has 434 condos and was designed by the late Rafael Viñoly, with Coastal Construction as general contractor.

The developers secured an $817 million construction loan from Tyko Capital, arranged by Walker & Dunlop, which the article describes as the largest condo construction financing in Florida history. The developers say about 45% of the residences are sold, including a duplex penthouse under contract for $30 million. Plans include a private club on levels 61 to 64 and more than 300 feet of private dockage, with completion expected in 2030.

My take: A construction loan this size tells you lenders still believe in well-located luxury condos, even while older resale condos sit on the market. For preconstruction buyers, the groundbreaking and the financing are the two milestones that matter most, because they make delivery far more likely. Before signing on any new tower, read the deposit schedule, the rental rules and what happens if the delivery date slips. And remember that 2030 is a long time to tie up a deposit, so plan your cash flow around it.

Source: Florida YIMBY

Mortgage Rates

2. Daily 30-year rates closed last week between 7.32% and 7.50%, well above Freddie Mac's 7.11% weekly average

Mortgage News Daily's index for the 30-year fixed rate ended Friday, November 13, at 7.50%, up 0.08 point, with the 15-year at 7.03%. Mortgage Research Center data for the same day, published by Fortune this morning, put the average 30-year conventional rate at 7.319% and the 15-year at 6.498%.

Freddie Mac's weekly survey, released November 12, averaged 7.11% for the 30-year and 6.45% for the 15-year, down from 7.18% and 6.57% the week before. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.24%. Because the weekly survey reflects applications from the prior days, it can trail what lenders are quoting now. The next Freddie Mac reading comes out Thursday.

My take: On a $500,000 30-year loan, principal and interest is about $3,364 a month at 7.11%, $3,434 at 7.319% and $3,496 at 7.50%. That's roughly $132 a month between the weekly headline and Friday's daily index. If you're under contract, ask your lender for a written, same-day quote before deciding when to lock. If you're still shopping, ask sellers for a credit toward a rate buydown; in a slower condo market, many will listen.

Source: Mortgage News Daily · Fortune (Mortgage Research Center data) · Freddie Mac PMMS · Freddie Mac release (GlobeNewswire)

Brickell

3. A 76-story B Residences tower is filed for Brickell, and Ytech lands $42 million for its second condo tower

Plans for B Residences Miami, an 834-foot, 76-story tower with 369 residences at 11 Southwest 7th Street in Brickell, have been submitted to the city's Urban Development Review Board, Florida YIMBY reported on November 15. The Arquitectonica-designed project from Empira Group and Partners Group includes 707 parking spaces and about 14,758 square feet of ground-floor retail, and the board review is scheduled for November 18.

A few blocks south, Ytech secured a $42 million pre-development loan from Linkvest Capital for 75 Broadway, a 52-story tower with 106 condos near Southwest 15th Road, Commercial Observer reported on November 13. Sales launched the week before, prices start at $3 million and completion is slated for late 2029. Ytech's founder said zoning allowed nearly 300 units, but the company chose to build 106 larger homes.

My take: Brickell's new pipeline is mostly larger, pricier units, not the small investor condos of the last cycle. That's good news for owners of well-kept existing buildings with reasonable HOA fees, because new product at $3 million and up doesn't compete with them directly. If you own an older Brickell unit, though, expect buyers to compare your building's reserves and assessments with brand-new towers. A plan filing is an early step; I don't treat a project as real until it has financing and a construction start.

Source: Florida YIMBY (B Residences) · Commercial Observer (75 Broadway)

Rentals

4. Ralgo Group buys the 267-unit Treetop Apartments for $53.5 million

An entity led by Amy and Richard Gonzalez of Miami-based Ralgo Group bought Treetop Apartments at 8520 Southwest 107th Avenue for $53.5 million, or about $200,375 per unit, The Real Deal reported in its November 12 roundup of top deals. The three-story complex has 267 units on a 9.2-acre site, was built in 1974 and has two pools. The seller bought it for $8.5 million in 1995, and units are listed at $1,750 a month, according to the report.

My take: Investors paying about $200,000 a door for a 1970s complex shows how much confidence there is in Miami's suburban rental demand. For owners of a rental condo or townhouse in southwest Miami-Dade, that demand helps support both rents and resale values. If you're an international buyer looking for income, compare a well-run suburban rental with a downtown condo: the price per unit, HOA fees and insurance often make the numbers work better away from the bay.

Source: The Real Deal (top deals)

Week Ahead

5. Miami-Dade's October sales report is due today, with a Brickell board vote Wednesday and new rates Thursday

MIAMI Realtors' release schedule lists October existing-home sales for November 16. As of early this morning the figures had not been posted on its news page, so the first full look at October prices, sales and inventory for Miami-Dade houses and condos should come later today.

Also this week: the Urban Development Review Board is scheduled to review B Residences on Wednesday, November 18, and Freddie Mac publishes its next weekly mortgage rate survey on Thursday.

My take: When the October numbers come out, the split between houses and condos is what I'll watch, because the two markets are moving in different directions right now. If you want the numbers for your own zip code and property type, send me a WhatsApp and I'll pull them for you.

Source: MIAMI Realtors release schedule · MIAMI Realtors news releases

Bottom line

Lenders are still putting big money behind new luxury condos on the river and in Brickell, while daily mortgage rates ended last week near 7.5%. If you're buying, lock with a real quote, not the weekly headline. Today's October report will show how the rest of the Miami-Dade market is holding up.
