Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 28, 2026: New Home Sales Jump 14% as Rates Hold Near 7.1% and a $140M Loan Backs 363 Aventura Rentals

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 28, 2026

Good morning, Miami. It's the quiet Saturday after Thanksgiving, which makes it a good morning to catch up on what happened while we were all at the table. Builders had their best month in a while, mortgage rates are holding near their lowest level this month heading into a big week of data, and investors kept putting money into Aventura and Wynwood. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. Rates hold near the month's lows heading into jobs week

The Optimal Blue daily index for a 30-year fixed mortgage stood at 7.14%, a reading from November 24 that was carried through the holiday, Mortgage Daily reported on Friday. That is down from 7.29% on November 20 and the lowest reading since November 9. The 15-year sat at 6.53%. Zillow data published by Yahoo Finance on Friday put the 30-year at 7.18%. Freddie Mac's weekly average, released Wednesday, was 7.21%.

The coming days could move things. Mortgage Daily lists Freddie Mac's next weekly survey on Thursday, the November jobs report on December 4, the Federal Reserve's decision on December 9 and November inflation data on December 10. The same report says futures markets are pricing a quarter-point Fed rate increase in December as close to certain, and Fed officials entered their pre-meeting quiet period today.

My take: On a $500,000, 30-year loan, principal and interest at 7.14% is about $3,374 a month, roughly $51 less than at the 7.29% we saw a week earlier. If you're under contract and closing in December, talk to your lender this weekend about locking before Friday's jobs report, because a surprise in either direction can move rates in a day. If you're still shopping, the bigger lever right now is the seller: December is slow, and many will pay toward a rate buydown to close before year-end.

Source: Mortgage Daily, Nov. 27 · Yahoo Finance (Zillow data), Nov. 27

New Construction

2. New home sales jump 14.2% in October while prices slip

Sales of new single-family homes ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 701,000 in October, 14.2% above September's 614,000 and 7.5% above October 2025, the Census Bureau reported on November 25. The median new home sold for $391,300, down 0.3% from September and 3.2% from a year earlier, while the average price fell 12.1% from a year ago to $459,900.

There were about 470,000 new homes for sale at the end of October, 4.1% fewer than a year earlier, which equals 8.0 months of supply at the current sales pace, down from 9.3 months in September. The Census Bureau notes that monthly figures carry wide margins of error and are often revised.

My take: Lower prices seem to be helping builders sell more homes, and that matters in Miami-Dade too, where much of the new single-family construction is in the western and southern suburbs. If you're comparing a resale house with a new one, ask the builder exactly what incentive they offer for a closing this quarter and put it in writing. A builder-paid rate buydown can beat a few thousand dollars off the price, but only if you plan to keep the loan for years.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, New Residential Sales, Nov. 25

Aventura

3. A $140 million loan backs Spoke Aventura, a 363-unit Live Local tower

Group P6, a Boca Raton developer, has secured a $140 million construction loan from RXR for Spoke Aventura, a 21-story, 363-unit rental tower at 2600 NE 203rd Street built under Florida's Live Local Act, The American Developer reported on November 25, citing Commercial Observer. The 1.3-acre site, bought in 2024 for $16 million, sits a block west of Biscayne Boulevard near the Brightline station, about a mile north of Aventura Mall.

Plans include a 435-space garage with ground-floor retail, a padel court, pool deck, gym and coworking space. More than 40% of the units are planned for households earning up to 120% of the area median income, Moss is the general contractor, and completion is targeted for 2028, according to the report.

My take: Aventura has been a condo town for decades, and this is a sign that rentals near the Brightline station are now a serious bet. If you own a condo in Aventura that you rent out, plan for more competition around 2028, especially for one- and two-bedrooms. If you're a buyer who wants a train to Fort Lauderdale and Miami at your door, the blocks near the station are worth watching as they fill in.

Source: The American Developer, Nov. 25

Wynwood

4. Thor Equities pays $11.3 million for more Wynwood land

Thor Equities, the New York firm led by Joe Sitt, has bought a vacant 26,100-square-foot parcel at 2521 NW First Avenue and 74 NW 26th Street in Wynwood for $11.3 million and plans a retail and residential project valued at $180 million, The American Developer reported on November 25, citing Commercial Observer. That works out to roughly $433 per square foot of land.

Thor built the Wynwood Walk retail building at 2800 NW Second Avenue in 2020 and has kept buying nearby, including a 1.1-acre assemblage at 2825 NW Second Avenue for $20.2 million and a parcel at 2724 NW Second Avenue for $10 million, according to the report. The company has not released an architect, unit count or timeline for the new site.

My take: When a big investor keeps buying block after block in the same neighborhood, it's betting on where the foot traffic will be in five or ten years. For buyers looking at Wynwood or nearby Edgewater and Midtown, that's a vote of confidence. For owners of small lots or older buildings in the area, land values like these are a reason to get a current valuation before you accept the first offer that comes in.

Source: The American Developer, Nov. 25

Permits

5. November's biggest South Florida permits total $473 million

The ten largest building permits issued in South Florida between October 27 and November 26 carry about $473 million in declared construction cost, with four multifamily projects accounting for $284.6 million, according to a November 26 roundup by The American Developer based on City of Miami and Miami-Dade County permit data.

The list includes a $100 million multifamily permit at 7940 West Drive, a $65 million retail project for the City of Miami Gardens, a $50 million permit for Miami Country Day School and a $34 million permit for Gulliver Preparatory. The report notes that permit values are the applicant's declared cost, not a full budget, and a permit is not a groundbreaking.

My take: Two private schools pulling a combined $84 million in permits is a detail families should notice: schools invest where they expect enrollment to grow. If you're relocating with kids, school choice drives where you'll want to live, and in Miami the right school can matter more than the right building. Tell me the schools you're considering and I'll show you which neighborhoods make the commute easy.

Source: The American Developer, Nov. 26

Miami real estate questions people are asking

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Freddie Mac's weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.21% as of November 25, 2026, and the Optimal Blue daily index was 7.14% in its latest reading. Miami rates track national averages; yours depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

Are new homes cheaper than they were a year ago?

Nationally, yes. The median new home sold for $391,300 in October 2026, 3.2% less than a year earlier, according to the Census Bureau. Many builders are also offering rate buydowns and closing-cost help, so compare incentives, not just prices.

What is a Live Local Act building in Miami?

Florida's Live Local Act lets developers build apartments on land zoned for commercial or industrial use when a share of the units is set aside for workforce households, typically those earning up to 120% of the area median income. Spoke Aventura, a 363-unit tower near the Aventura Brightline station, is one example.

Is December a good time to buy a home in Miami?

It can be. Fewer buyers shop over the holidays, and sellers who want to close before year-end are often more flexible on price, credits or rate buydowns. Watch the December 4 jobs report and the December 9 Fed decision, which can move mortgage rates.

Bottom line

Rates are holding near this month's lows, builders are moving homes with lower prices and incentives, and investors are still betting on Aventura and Wynwood. The next two weeks bring the jobs report and the Fed, so if you're buying, have your lender and your lock plan lined up now.
