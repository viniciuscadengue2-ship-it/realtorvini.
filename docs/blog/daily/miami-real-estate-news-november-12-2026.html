Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 12, 2026: Miami's First Supertall Nears Completion as Mortgage Rates Tick Up After Inflation Data

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 12, 2026

Good morning, Miami. Today's edition covers a small rate bump after the October inflation report, the tower that is changing our skyline, a busy week at the top of the market, and a county loan program that every condo owner facing an assessment should know about. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. Daily mortgage rates edge up after a hotter-than-expected October inflation report

Daily rate trackers moved higher this week. Zillow data provided to Yahoo Finance put the average 30-year fixed purchase rate at 6.98% as of November 10, up 5 basis points from the day before but still 12 basis points lower than a week earlier, with the 15-year at 6.41%. Yahoo Finance tied the rise to a higher-than-expected October inflation report. Mortgage Research Center data reviewed by Fortune showed the 30-year at 7.107% and the 15-year at 6.273% for the same day; the two trackers use different lenders and methods, so their numbers never match exactly.

The Federal Reserve left its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4.00% at its October 27-28 meeting, and its last meeting of the year is December 8-9. Forecasts quoted by Yahoo Finance have the 30-year averaging 7.3% this quarter (Mortgage Bankers Association) and 7.4% (Fannie Mae). Freddie Mac publishes its weekly average later today.

My take: Small moves, real money: on a $500,000 30-year loan, principal and interest is about $3,320 a month at 6.98%, $3,363 at 7.107% and $3,428 at 7.3%. Rates vary a lot from lender to lender, so I always tell buyers to get at least two or three written quotes on the same day. If you're under contract, talk to your lender about locking. If you're still shopping, a seller credit toward a rate buydown is often worth more than a small price cut.

Source: Yahoo Finance (Zillow data) · Fortune (Mortgage Research Center data)

New Development

2. Waldorf Astoria Miami is on track to become Florida's first supertall, and more are lining up behind it

The 100-story Waldorf Astoria Hotel & Residences across from Bayside Marketplace is expected to be completed in 2028 at 1,049 feet, which would make it the first building in Florida over the 984-foot 'supertall' mark, WLRN reported. Developer PMG says the vast majority of its 360 condos are already sold; the tower also includes a 205-room hotel.

Several more very tall towers are planned nearby, including One Bayfront Tower and Miami RiverBridge, both expected around 2030, and 888 Brickell, expected in 2029. WLRN notes none will rise above the Waldorf, because of the federal height limit tied to Miami International Airport's flight path.

My take: Projects like this put downtown Miami on the map for international buyers, and that attention tends to help the whole area, not just one tower. If you're looking at preconstruction, remember the timeline: you sign today and close years from now, so read the deposit schedule and the developer's track record carefully. If you want resale you can live in now, downtown and Edgewater have finished buildings with history you can check. I'm happy to compare both with you.

Source: WLRN

Luxury

3. Miami-Dade's $4 million-plus market signs 16 contracts in a week, led by Surf Club and Fisher Island

Sixteen Miami-Dade properties asking $4 million or more went under contract from November 2 to 8, with a combined asking price of $136.4 million, up from 11 contracts the week before, according to Douglas Elliman's Eklund-Gomes team as reported by The Real Deal. The biggest were a Surf Club condo at 9001 Collins Avenue at $21.9 million and a Palazzo Della Luna unit on Fisher Island at $18.9 million. Condos in the group spent an average of 208 days on the market; houses averaged 104 days.

At the very top, a three-property Palm Island compound in Miami Beach is now asking $185.5 million, the New York Post reported. A sale at that price would beat the $170 million Indian Creek Island purchase in March as the county's residential record.

My take: The gap in days on market tells the story: luxury houses are moving about twice as fast as luxury condos. Sellers of high-end condos need pricing and presentation that stand out, because buyers have time and choices. For my international clients, the steady pace at $4 million and up shows global money is still choosing Miami, which supports values further down the price range too.

Source: The Real Deal · New York Post via Yahoo

Condos

4. Facing a special assessment? Miami-Dade offers condo owners up to $50,000 at 0% interest

Speaking at The Real Deal's Miami Real Estate Forum, Miami-Dade Mayor Daniella Levine Cava said the county's surtax-funded loan program offers eligible condo households up to $50,000 at zero interest over 40 years, for families earning up to 140% of area median income. She said it has helped more than 2,000 families and distributed about $53 million.

She also said the county has caught up on building recertifications and created a registry that requires condo associations to disclose board information and building reports.

My take: This is one of the most useful and least known tools for owners hit by post-Surfside repair assessments. A $50,000 loan at 0% over 40 years works out to about $104 a month. If you own a condo and an assessment is coming, check whether you qualify before you think about selling in a hurry. If you're buying, ask whether the building has a pending assessment and whether the seller plans to pay it before closing.

Source: The Real Deal

Brickell

5. Preservation board clears the way for a Nobu condo tower behind First Miami Presbyterian Church

Miami's historic preservation board voted 6-1 on November 3 to lift the historic designation from the church's former school and parking lot at 609 Brickell Avenue, shrinking the protected area to the 1949 church and a 15-foot buffer, according to a Miami Herald editorial. The decision supports the planned 619 Brickell by Nobu condo tower tied to a $240 million land sale, which the Herald says looks on track for completion around 2030 or 2031. It is not yet clear whether opponents will appeal.

My take: Brickell keeps adding branded luxury towers, which brings more choice for buyers and more competition for owners of older buildings nearby. If you own in Brickell and plan to sell in the next few years, the time to invest in your unit's condition and your building's paperwork is now, before the new supply arrives.

Source: Miami Herald editorial via Yahoo

Miami real estate questions people are asking

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Daily trackers put the average 30-year fixed rate at 6.98% (Zillow) to 7.107% (Mortgage Research Center) as of November 10, 2026. Freddie Mac publishes its weekly average on Thursday, November 12. Miami rates track national averages; your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type, so compare quotes from several lenders.

What is the tallest building in Miami?

The Waldorf Astoria Hotel & Residences in downtown Miami is set to reach 1,049 feet and 100 stories, with completion expected in 2028. It will be Florida's first supertall building, meaning taller than 984 feet.

Is there help for Miami condo owners with special assessments?

Yes. Miami-Dade's surtax-funded loan program offers eligible condo households up to $50,000 at 0% interest over 40 years, for incomes up to 140% of area median income, according to the county mayor. Check eligibility with the county before making decisions about your unit.

Is the Miami luxury market still active?

Yes. Sixteen Miami-Dade properties asking $4 million or more went under contract from November 2 to 8, 2026, with luxury houses selling about twice as fast as luxury condos.

Bottom line

Rates bumped up a little this week but are still close to their fall lows, downtown Miami is about to get its first supertall, and the luxury market keeps signing deals. If you own a condo with an assessment coming, look into the county's 0% loan before you decide anything. If you're buying, compare lender quotes and check the building's paperwork first.
