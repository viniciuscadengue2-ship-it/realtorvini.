Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 13, 2026: Mortgage Rates Fall to 7.11% as Miami Condo Sellers Outnumber Buyers Nearly 4 to 1

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 13, 2026

Good morning, Miami. Rates slipped for a fourth week, a new Redfin report shows just how much negotiating power condo buyers have in Miami right now, and the top of our market keeps moving in the opposite direction of the rest of the country. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. The 30-year rate drops to 7.11%, its lowest in seven weeks and fourth weekly decline in a row

Freddie Mac's weekly survey released Thursday put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.11%, down from 7.18% the week before, and the 15-year at 6.45%, down from 6.57%. A year ago the averages were 6.24% and 5.49%. Freddie Mac's chief economist Sam Khater called it the lowest level in seven weeks.

The relief may be short-lived. RISMedia reported that Mortgage News Daily's daily index was back up to 7.42% by Thursday afternoon, with October inflation running hotter than forecast and the 10-year Treasury yield pushing toward 5%. Bright MLS chief economist Lisa Sturtevant told RISMedia she sees little reason to expect rates to fall further this year.

My take: On a $500,000 30-year loan, principal and interest is about $3,364 a month at 7.11%, roughly $24 less than last week's $3,387. At the 7.42% daily reading it would be about $3,469. Weekly averages lag what lenders are quoting today, so if you're under contract and happy with a quote, talk to your lender about locking rather than waiting for next Thursday's number. If you're shopping, seller credits toward a buydown are easier to win right now, especially on condos.

Source: Freddie Mac PMMS · Freddie Mac release (GlobeNewswire) · RISMedia

Condos

2. Miami has 274% more condo sellers than buyers, the third-widest gap among the 50 largest U.S. metros

Redfin counted about 10,234 condo sellers and 2,739 condo buyers in the Miami metro in September, or 274% more sellers than buyers, nearly four for every buyer. Only San Antonio and Houston were more lopsided. Fort Lauderdale (192%) and West Palm Beach (109%) also favored buyers. Nationally, sellers outnumbered buyers by 123%, the widest gap in Redfin's records going back to 2013.

Across the U.S., 37% of condos that sold in September had a price cut, and the typical condo took 63 days to go under contract, compared with 35 days five years ago. A Miami Redfin agent quoted in the report said the area has enough condo inventory for the next three to four years.

My take: Miami condos are in a clear buyer's market right now. Buyers: ask for price reductions, seller-paid closing costs and help with any pending assessment, and use the inspection period to dig into the building's reserves, insurance and milestone reports. Sellers: price against the cheapest comparable unit in your own building, not the best sale from two years ago, and make your paperwork easy for a buyer's lender to approve. International buyers paying cash have real leverage right now.

Source: Redfin · Real Estate News

Luxury

3. Luxury prices are falling nationally but rising in Miami

To be in the top 10% of listings in the Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro in October, a home had to be priced at about $2.3 million, up 4.1% from a year earlier, according to Realtor.com's monthly luxury report. Nationally, that entry point fell 2.3% to $1,197,014, the 31st straight month of annual declines.

Miami was one of 12 metros out of 43 where the luxury threshold rose at least 1%, and five of them are in Florida, led by Naples-Marco Island.

My take: Miami's split market in one picture: entry-level condos are crowded with sellers while the high end keeps getting more expensive, because land, water frontage and newer single-family homes are scarce. If you own a well-located house, you're in a strong position. If you're a buyer stretching for the luxury tier, the better values are often in condos, where sellers are negotiating.

Source: Realtor.com (PR Newswire) · CiberCuba

Big Deals

4. Juwan Howard's Coral Gables home sells for $25 million, and a Kendall apartment complex trades for $53.5 million

Attorney Patrick Collins paid $25 million, or $2,777 per square foot, for Juwan Howard's 9,000-square-foot home at 9440 Old Cutler Lane in Coral Gables, The Real Deal reported. The home, on 1.7 acres, went under contract in September at $28.9 million; Howard bought it in 2013 for $3.4 million. In Golden Beach, a 2024-built waterfront home at 342 South Parkway sold for $14.3 million.

On the rental side, Ralgo Group paid $53.5 million, or $200,375 per unit, for the 267-unit Treetop Apartments at 8520 SW 107th Avenue in Kendall, a three-story complex built in 1974 on 9.2 acres, according to The Real Deal and CRE News.

My take: The Howard sale closed about 13% below its contract price, a reminder that even at the top, final numbers get negotiated. For investors, a buyer paying around $200,000 per unit for a 1970s Kendall complex shows how much demand there is for rental housing in the suburbs. If you're thinking about a small multifamily or a rental condo, I can run the numbers with you.

Source: The Real Deal · CRE News

Brickell

5. A Romanian developer puts $43 million into ORA by Casa Tua, the planned 77-story Brickell tower

Fortune International Group announced that Bucharest-listed One United Properties has joined ORA by Casa Tua as a joint-venture partner, investing $43 million in the tower planned for 1210 Brickell Avenue, PROFILEmiami reported. The project calls for 533 fully furnished residences, from studios to four bedrooms, with sales underway and construction scheduled to start in the first quarter of 2027.

My take: Foreign developers putting their own capital into Brickell is a vote of confidence in Miami as a global city. For preconstruction buyers, a construction start date is the milestone to watch: read the deposit schedule, ask where deposits are held and plan for a delivery several years out. If you want something to live in or rent now, Miami's resale condo market is where buyers have the upper hand.

Source: PROFILEmiami

Miami real estate questions people are asking

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Freddie Mac's national weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.11% on November 12, 2026, down from 7.18% a week earlier, and 6.45% for a 15-year fixed. Daily indexes moved back up later that day. Miami rates track national averages; your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

Is Miami a buyer's market for condos?

Yes. Redfin counted 274% more condo sellers than buyers in the Miami metro in September 2026, nearly four sellers for every buyer and the third-widest gap among the 50 largest U.S. metros. Buyers have room to negotiate on price and closing costs.

Are Miami luxury home prices going up?

Yes. Realtor.com found the entry price for the top 10% of listings in the Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro rose 4.1% from a year earlier to about $2.3 million in October 2026, while the national figure fell 2.3%.

How much is the monthly payment on a $500,000 mortgage in Miami?

At the November 12, 2026 Freddie Mac average of 7.11%, principal and interest on a $500,000 30-year loan is about $3,364 a month, before property taxes, insurance and any HOA fees.

Bottom line

Rates eased to 7.11% but may bounce, Miami condos are firmly a buyer's market, and luxury homes keep getting pricier. If you're buying a condo, negotiate hard and check the building first. If you're selling a house in a good location, the market is still on your side.
