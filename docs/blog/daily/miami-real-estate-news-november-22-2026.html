Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 22, 2026: Record Rain Floods Broward as King Tides Return and a Judge Orders a Condo Review

I also look at a Hialeah Gardens condo where a judge just ordered an outside review, the entry prices on new condo projects, and how far daily mortgage rates fell over the week. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Flooding

1. Record rain floods Broward neighborhoods, and king tides return this week

Heavy rain that began Tuesday night, November 17, dropped more than 15 inches on parts of Broward over the week, CBS News Miami reported. NBC6 reported that Fort Lauderdale recorded 5.72 inches in one day, breaking a daily record of 1.8 inches set in 1959, and WSVN said the city was having its wettest November on record. Broward County, Fort Lauderdale and Hollywood declared emergencies. In Hollywood, more than 110 people were rescued, and about 30 Broward schools reported flooding or damage, keeping students home until after Thanksgiving. CBS reported no injuries or deaths connected to the flooding.

Hollywood Mayor Josh Levy told WSVN that "nothing is designed to handle a rain event like this" and urged residents to photograph and report their losses for possible state and federal help. Miami-Dade schools reopened on Friday. The next king tide window is November 23 to 26, according to Miami-Dade County, which lists the dates from the South Florida Water Management District. NBC6 also noted that the National Weather Service expects a wetter than normal dry season, with a greater than 60% chance of above-normal rain from January to April.

My take: If you're buying, ask about water before you fall in love with a house: the flood zone, the elevation certificate, whether the street floods, and what the current owner pays for flood insurance. FEMA says flood policies typically take 30 days to go into effect after purchase, so don't wait for the next storm to buy one. If you have Citizens, most policyholders will need flood coverage to renew starting January 1, 2027, condo units excepted. If you're selling, keep your records of any water damage and repairs organized, because buyers will ask. If you need an insurance agent or a contractor who answers the phone, send me a WhatsApp and I'll share the people I trust.

Condo Governance

2. A judge appoints an outside receiver to review a 635-unit Hialeah Gardens condo

Miami-Dade Circuit Judge Valerie Manno Schurr appointed a receiver, Francis Trullenque, to review the condition and finances of Samari Lake East Condominium in Hialeah Gardens, NBC6 reported on November 19. The community has 635 apartments. A unit owner sued the association in October over water damage, and owners celebrated the ruling in the management office, according to the report.

The case comes after the head of Sunshine Management Services and five others were arrested this summer, accused of stealing a combined $5.8 million from five condo associations, including Samari Lake East. Five of the defendants have pleaded not guilty, and no board members have been charged, NBC6 reported. The association's attorney told NBC6 that "the association is well funded." The receiver is expected to present initial findings at a court hearing.

My take: This is why I read condo documents line by line before my clients commit. Before you buy, ask for the last two years of board minutes, the budget, the reserve study or structural integrity reserve study, the milestone inspection status, and any pending lawsuits, and ask who the management company is. Unexplained gaps in the money or a board that won't answer questions are red flags, whatever the view. If you own in a building and something feels off, you have the right to request records; talk to a condo attorney about your options.

Source: NBC6 Investigates

New Development

3. What it costs to get into Miami's new condo projects, from $758,900 to $15 million

Haute Residence's November pre-construction roundup, updated November 20, lists building-level starting prices for South Florida projects. In Miami, Delano Residences in downtown starts at $758,900 with delivery listed for 2031, Jean Georges Miami Tropic Residences in the Design District at $1.1 million, THE WELL Coconut Grove at $1.5 million and Cipriani Residences in Brickell at $1.6 million. At the top, Four Seasons Private Residences Coconut Grove is listed from $5.62 million and The Residences at Six Fisher Island from $15 million.

The same report notes that the Residences at Mandarin Oriental on Brickell Key broke ground on October 1 with more than $1.7 billion in presales, and that Villa Miami in Edgewater topped out at 56 stories in late October. Haute Residence cautions that its figures are building-level starting prices, not prices for any specific residence, and some developer websites list different numbers.

My take: Starting prices tell you which buyer a building is for, but the real cost is in the deposit schedule and the delivery date. Many of these projects deliver in 2028 to 2031, so read the deposit schedule first and be sure you're comfortable with your money tied up until closing. Compare that with resale condos in the same neighborhoods, where there is more supply right now and sellers negotiate. If you're deciding between new and resale, I'll put both side by side for you, including fees and insurance.

Source: Haute Residence, South Florida Pre-Construction Monthly

Mortgage Rates

4. Daily 30-year rates fell every day of the week, from 7.51% to 7.33%

Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed index declined each day last week, from 7.51% on Monday, November 16, to 7.33% on Friday, November 20, when the site reported rates "much lower." The index was 6.36% a year earlier. Freddie Mac's weekly average, released Thursday, was 7.28%, and the Mortgage Bankers Association's survey was 7.35% for the week reported November 18.

My take: On a $500,000 30-year loan, principal and interest is about $3,438 a month at 7.33%, roughly $61 less than at Monday's 7.51% ($3,499). That's a real saving, but not a reason to rush. Holiday weeks are usually quiet, so if you're under contract, get a written quote early in the week and decide on a lock with your lender rather than waiting for headlines.

Source: Mortgage News Daily, 30-year fixed index

Miami real estate questions people are asking

Did the November 2026 flooding affect Miami-Dade?

The worst flooding was in Broward, where parts of the county got more than 15 inches of rain in the week of November 17, 2026. Miami-Dade schools reopened on November 20. King tides are expected again from November 23 to 26, according to Miami-Dade County.

How long does flood insurance take to start in Florida?

FEMA says National Flood Insurance Program policies typically have a 30-day waiting period from the date of purchase. Buy coverage before you need it and ask your agent about exceptions.

What should I check before buying a Miami condo?

Review the budget, reserves, structural integrity reserve study, milestone inspection status, board minutes, pending lawsuits and the management company. Ask a condo attorney if anything is unclear.

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Mortgage News Daily's index put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.33% on November 20, 2026, and Freddie Mac's weekly average was 7.28% on November 19. Your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

Bottom line

This week's floods in Broward are a reminder to check water risk and flood insurance before you buy, and king tides return November 23 to 26. In condos, read the documents and the finances, not just the listing. Daily rates fell all week to 7.33%, a small break heading into Thanksgiving.
