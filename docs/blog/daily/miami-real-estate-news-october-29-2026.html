Miami Real Estate News, Oct. 29, 2026: Mortgage Rates Hit an October Low as Miami Inventory Keeps Shrinking

By Vinicius Cadengue, Compass · October 29, 2026

Good morning, Miami. Here's what moved in our housing market this week, in about four minutes: rates finally gave buyers a small break, inventory keeps tightening for houses, the luxury market is setting records, and condo buyers are doing more homework than ever. Below each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. Mortgage rates drop for a second straight week, to the lowest level this month

Freddie Mac's weekly survey released today puts the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.24%, down from 7.32% a week ago, with the 15-year at 6.60%. That's the lowest 30-year reading of October, though still well above the 6.17% average of a year ago. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate steady on Wednesday, as markets expected, and purchase mortgage applications ticked up week over week while remaining below last year's pace.

My take: On a $500,000 loan, this week's dip saves roughly $27 a month in principal and interest. Small, but it's moving the right way. If you're buying, don't try to time the bottom; get pre-approved now and use today's softer condo market to negotiate seller credits toward a rate buydown. International buyers using foreign national loans or paying cash should still watch this, because it shapes how much local competition you'll face.

Source: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey

Miami-Dade Market

2. Miami-Dade inventory falls for an eighth straight month, and house prices are still climbing

The latest MIAMI Realtors report (September data) shows total active listings down about 10% from a year earlier, to 16,242, with single-family inventory down nearly 17%. Total sales slipped 2% to 1,733, while the single-family median price rose about 4% to $692,000. Sellers of houses received a median of 95% of their original list price; condo sellers got about 94%.

My take: This is really two markets. Houses are tightening: fewer listings and rising prices, so well-priced homes in areas like Kendall, South Miami and the Gables still move. Condos are where buyers have the leverage. Sellers, that 95% figure is your reality check: price it right on day one instead of chasing the market down.

Source: MIAMI Realtors, September 2026 Miami-Dade report

Luxury

3. Miami-Dade breaks its record for $30 million-plus home sales with three months to spare

Through September the county logged 24 single-family and condo sales of $30 million or more, already beating the full-year 2025 record of 21. Cash buyers made up just over 38% of all Miami-Dade closings in September, far above the national share.

My take: High-net-worth buyers, many from out of state and abroad, keep choosing Miami. For my Brazilian and Latin American clients, the takeaway isn't the $30M headlines; it's that global money still sees Miami as a safe place to hold dollar-denominated real estate, which supports long-term values at every price point.

Source: MIAMI Realtors via PR Newswire

Condos

4. Condo resales show signs of life, but buyers and lenders are scrutinizing the buildings

Miami Today reports that Miami-Dade's condo resale market is improving, but buyers and lenders are looking much more closely at the building behind each unit: its finances, reserves and inspection history. Countywide, condo supply is running around 13 months at the current sales pace, versus under 6 months for houses.

My take: Before you fall in love with the view, ask for the building's milestone inspection report, its structural integrity reserve study (SIRS), recent board minutes, and any planned special assessments. Those documents now decide whether a lender will finance the unit and what you'll really pay each month. I review them with every condo buyer I represent.

Source: Miami Today

Local News

5. Faster home permits advance in Miami-Dade; Lincoln Road redevelopment gets the green light

Miami-Dade's Policy Council advanced legislation that would require the county to set up a same-day permitting program for some residential applications. Separately, Miami Beach commissioners gave final approval on Oct. 14 to a plan to redevelop part of the Lincoln Road district as a new retail and dining destination.

My take: Same-day permits would be a big deal for buyers who plan to renovate. Permit delays are one of the biggest hidden costs I see on older homes in Kendall and the Grove. And upgrades like Lincoln Road tend to lift nearby condo demand over time.

Source: Miami Today / Florida Trend

Miami real estate questions people are asking today

Is Miami a buyer's or seller's market right now?

It depends on the property type. Single-family houses in Miami-Dade are roughly balanced, with under 6 months of supply and rising prices. Condos are a buyer's market, with about 13 months of supply countywide, so buyers have room to negotiate.

What are mortgage rates today?

Freddie Mac's weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.24% as of October 29, 2026, and 6.60% for a 15-year fixed. Your actual rate depends on your credit, down payment and loan type.

Are home prices dropping in Miami?

Not for houses. The Miami-Dade single-family median rose about 4% year over year to $692,000 in September 2026. Condo prices have been roughly flat, and some buildings are selling at a discount.

How many Miami home buyers pay cash?

About 38% of Miami-Dade home sales closed in cash in September 2026, well above the national average.

Bottom line

Rates are easing a little, houses are getting scarcer, and condos are a negotiation. If you're thinking about buying, the best opportunities right now are well-reviewed condo buildings with strong reserves. If you're selling a house, low inventory is on your side, as long as you price it right.
