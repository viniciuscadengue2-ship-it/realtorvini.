Miami Real Estate News, Nov. 26, 2026: Mortgage Rates Ease to 7.21% for Thanksgiving as 1,552 Rentals Advance in Brickell and Downtown

By Vinicius Cadengue, Miami Realtor · Riley Smith Group at Compass · November 26, 2026

Good morning, Miami, and happy Thanksgiving. Freddie Mac moved its weekly rate survey up a day for the holiday, and the news is modestly good: rates came down. Lenders and the city also kept two big rental towers moving, one in Brickell and one on Biscayne Boulevard, and a small Wynwood project shows a different way to house the people who work in our restaurants. Under each story is what I'm telling my own clients.

Mortgage Rates

1. Freddie Mac's 30-year average falls to 7.21% ahead of the holiday

The 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged 7.21% as of November 25, down from 7.28% the week before, according to Freddie Mac's weekly survey, which was published on Wednesday because of Thanksgiving. A year ago the average was 6.23%. The 15-year fixed fell to 6.54% from 6.66%. Freddie Mac chief economist Sam Khater said the drop was "offering modest relief for prospective homebuyers."

Buyers seem to have noticed. Mortgage applications to buy a home rose 5% in the week ending November 20, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association, though they were still about 16% below a year earlier, Realtor.com News reported. The same report cited Realtor.com data showing the typical home was listed for about 2.2% less in October than a year before.

My take: On a $500,000 30-year loan, principal and interest at 7.21% comes to about $3,397 a month, roughly $24 less than at last week's 7.28%, but about $325 more than at last year's 6.23%. So this is relief, not a turnaround. Where I see real savings right now is negotiation: with fewer buyers out over the holidays, many Miami sellers will consider paying for a rate buydown or closing costs. If you're shopping, ask for it. If you're selling, offering a credit toward the buyer's rate can do more for you than a price cut of the same size.

Source: Seeking Alpha via TradingView, Nov. 25 · Realtor.com News via New American Funding, Nov. 25 · The Company Chronicle, Nov. 25

Brickell

2. A $345 million loan backs a 42-story, 1,008-apartment tower in Brickell

Namdar Group has closed a $345 million construction loan from SCALE Lending, the lending arm of Slate Property Group, for a 42-story rental tower at 250 SW 7th Street in Brickell, Florida YIMBY reported on November 24. The building is planned with 1,008 studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom apartments, about 2,600 square feet of ground-floor retail, 19,000 square feet of amenities on the ninth floor and 258 parking spaces.

The floating-rate loan has a 30-month term with a 12-month extension option. Behar Font & Partners is the architect, JMA is the general contractor, Related Group's management company will run the property, and completion is expected in May 2029, according to the report.

My take: A thousand new apartments with only 258 parking spaces tells you who the developer expects to rent there: young professionals who walk, take the Metromover or don't own a car. For investors who own a studio or one-bedroom condo in Brickell and rent it out, that's future competition, likely around 2029. It's one more reason to keep your unit updated and your building's finances in order. For renters, more supply in Brickell is good news for choice and, over time, for negotiating power.

Source: Florida YIMBY, Nov. 24

Downtown

3. 1775 Biscayne gets a $150 million building permit for 544 apartments

The City of Miami issued a construction permit on November 19 with a declared value of $150 million for LCOR's 42-story tower at 1775 Biscayne Boulevard in the Arts & Entertainment District, Florida YIMBY reported on November 23, citing municipal data. The mixed-use building is planned with 544 rental apartments, about 10,000 square feet of ground-floor retail and 620 parking spaces in its podium.

ODP Architecture & Design designed the tower and Coastal Construction is building it. LCOR bought the roughly 1.08-acre site for $49 million in 2022 and secured a $192.5 million construction loan from Natixis in July. Construction began earlier this year, and completion is expected in the fourth quarter of 2028.

My take: This stretch of Biscayne between downtown and Edgewater keeps filling in. For buyers, that's the case for owning nearby: more residents bring more shops, restaurants and foot traffic. For condo owners who rent out their units in the area, two big rental towers delivering in 2028 and 2029 mean landlords will compete on finishes, price and flexibility. If you're deciding whether to sell a downtown rental now or hold, let's look at your numbers together.

Source: Florida YIMBY, Nov. 23

Wynwood

4. A 1926 Wynwood building adds 10 affordable units for hospitality workers

Kush Hospitality has converted a 1926 building at 2003 North Miami Avenue in Wynwood into a 7,500-square-foot mixed-use project with 10 residential micro-units for hospitality workers above a 50-seat restaurant, Broth and Bitters, plus a cocktail bar and a tattoo studio, Florida YIMBY reported on November 25.

The 10 units are covered by a 50-year affordability agreement. The project was done in partnership with the Omni Community Redevelopment Agency, with partial financial support from the Public Benefit Trust Fund, and the restaurant was scheduled to reopen this month after a two-year closure, according to the report.

My take: Ten apartments won't move Miami's rent numbers, but this is the kind of small, practical project the city needs more of: housing for the people who work in the neighborhood, inside a building that already exists. If you own an older commercial building in Wynwood, Little River or Allapattah, it's worth asking whether apartments upstairs and an incentive from the local redevelopment agency could make the numbers work. I'm happy to connect you with people who do these conversions.

Source: Florida YIMBY, Nov. 25

Miami real estate questions people are asking

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Freddie Mac's weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.21% as of November 25, 2026, down from 7.28% the week before. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.54%. Miami rates track national averages; yours depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

How much is the monthly payment on a $500,000 mortgage at 7.21%?

Principal and interest on a $500,000, 30-year fixed loan at 7.21% is about $3,397 a month. Property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA or condo fees come on top of that, and in Miami they can add a lot.

Is Thanksgiving a good time to buy a home in Miami?

It can be. Fewer buyers are out during the holidays, so there is often less competition and sellers may be more open to credits for closing costs or a rate buydown. Mortgage applications did rise 5% in the week before Thanksgiving, so serious buyers are still active.

Are new rental buildings being built in Brickell and downtown Miami?

Yes. Namdar Group closed a $345 million loan in November 2026 for a 1,008-apartment tower at 250 SW 7th Street in Brickell, expected in May 2029, and LCOR received a permit for 544 apartments at 1775 Biscayne Boulevard, expected in late 2028.

Bottom line

Rates eased to 7.21% for the holiday, a small but welcome break, and buyers who are out this week face less competition. Developers are still betting on renters in Brickell and downtown, which matters if you own a condo you rent out. Enjoy the day with your family; the market will be here on Friday.
