Miami-Dade Market

1. October sales fell 10.6%, but house prices kept rising and homes sold faster

Miami-Dade recorded 1,689 home sales in October, down 10.6% from 1,890 a year earlier, according to MIAMI Realtors' monthly report released November 16. Single-family sales fell 11.8% to 782 and condo sales fell 9.57% to 907. For the first ten months of the year, total sales are still up 4%, at 18,494 versus 17,790.

Prices split by property type. The single-family median rose 3.52% to $706,000, while the condo median slipped 0.63% to $397,500. Single-family listings dropped 13.81% to 4,739, leaving 5.4 months of supply, which the report calls a seller's market. Condo listings fell 5.30% to 11,989, but that is still 12.7 months of supply, a buyer's market. Houses went under contract in a median 42 days, down from 50, and sellers received a median 96% of their original list price; condos took 70 days and got 94%. Cash buyers made up 37.9% of all sales and 48.4% of condo sales. MIAMI Realtors' chief economist Gay Cororaton said sales are "likely to remain subdued through year-end."

My take: If you own a house, this is still your market: fewer listings, faster contracts and prices that keep inching up. Price it right on day one and you will likely get close to asking. If you're buying a condo, you have leverage. With a year of supply, ask for closing-cost credits, a rate buydown or repairs, and take your time with the building's reserve study, milestone inspection and insurance. For my Brazilian and Latin American clients paying cash, condos are where the negotiating room is right now. Want the October numbers for your own zip code? Send me a WhatsApp.

Property Taxes

2. Amendment 3 raises Florida's homestead exemption, and the residency cutoff is December 31

Florida voters approved Amendment 3 with about 62% of the vote, CBS12 reported. For non-school property taxes, it raises the homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, with inflation adjustments starting in 2029, according to Florida Realtors. School taxes are not included. For non-homestead property such as rentals, second homes and commercial buildings, the cap on yearly assessment increases for non-school taxes drops from 10% to 5%.

Timing matters. Both Florida Realtors and CBS12 report that homeowners who establish Florida residency by December 31, 2026, get the expanded exemption, while people who become residents later start with the existing exemption and get the larger benefit in their fifth year. MIAMI Realtors said in its October report that the change could bring more out-of-state buyers in the last two months of the year. CBS12 noted 2026 tax bills are not affected and that the details still depend on the Legislature.

My take: If you're planning to make Miami your primary home, the calendar is now part of the decision. Talk to a CPA or tax attorney soon about what establishing residency before December 31 would take in your case, because the rules are specific. If you're an international buyer or keeping Miami as a second home, you don't get the larger exemption, but the lower 5% cap on assessment increases helps make your future tax bills more predictable. Either way, I can help you estimate the property taxes on any home we look at.

Source: Florida Realtors · CBS12 · MIAMI Realtors

Mortgage Rates

3. Daily 30-year rates sit between 7.34% and 7.50% ahead of Thursday's Freddie Mac reading

Freddie Mac's latest weekly survey, released November 12, put the 30-year fixed average at 7.11%, down from 7.18% the week before, and the 15-year at 6.45%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.24%. The next weekly reading comes out Thursday.

Daily trackers are higher. Mortgage Research Center data for November 16, published by Fortune this morning, put the average 30-year conventional rate at 7.339% and the 15-year at 6.511%. Mortgage News Daily's index showed the 30-year at 7.50% for November 13, the latest close on its rate page.

My take: On a $500,000 30-year loan, principal and interest is about $3,364 a month at 7.11%, $3,441 at 7.339% and $3,496 at 7.50%. Put 20% down on October's median Miami-Dade house ($706,000) and the loan payment is about $3,799 a month at 7.11%, before taxes and insurance. If you're under contract, get a same-day written quote from your lender before you decide when to lock.

Source: Freddie Mac PMMS · Freddie Mac release (GlobeNewswire) · Fortune (Mortgage Research Center data) · Mortgage News Daily

New Construction

4. Faena Residences Miami breaks ground on twin 68-story towers on the Miami River with an $817 million loan

Construction has started on Faena Residences Miami, two 68-story towers rising about 800 feet at 24 Southwest 4th Street on the Miami River in downtown Miami, Florida YIMBY reported on November 13. The project from Fortune International Group and KAR Properties, in partnership with Faena, has 434 condos and is among the final designs of architect Rafael Viñoly. Coastal Construction is the general contractor.

The developers secured an $817 million construction loan from Tyko Capital, arranged by Walker & Dunlop, which the article describes as the largest condo construction financing in Florida history. About 45% of the residences are sold, including a duplex penthouse under contract for $30 million, and completion is expected in 2030.

My take: This is the other side of the condo story above. Resale condos have a year of supply, yet lenders are still putting record money behind new luxury towers in the right locations. For preconstruction buyers, a groundbreaking plus full construction financing are the milestones that make delivery much more likely. Before you sign on any new tower, read the deposit schedule, the rental rules and what happens if the delivery date slips, and plan your cash flow for a deposit tied up until 2030.

Source: Florida YIMBY

Miami real estate questions people are asking

Is Miami a buyer's or seller's market right now?

It depends on the property type. In October 2026, Miami-Dade single-family homes had 5.4 months of supply, which MIAMI Realtors calls a seller's market, while condos had 12.7 months of supply, a buyer's market.

What is the median home price in Miami-Dade?

In October 2026, the median sale price was $706,000 for single-family homes, up 3.52% from a year earlier, and $397,500 for condos, down 0.63%, according to MIAMI Realtors.

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Freddie Mac's national weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.11% on November 12, 2026, and 6.45% for a 15-year. Daily data was higher: 7.339% for the 30-year on November 16 (Mortgage Research Center). Your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

How much is the Florida homestead exemption under Amendment 3?

For non-school property taxes, Amendment 3 raises the homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, with inflation adjustments from 2029. Homeowners who establish Florida residency by December 31, 2026 get the expanded exemption; later residents get the larger benefit in their fifth year. Talk to a CPA or tax attorney about your situation.

Bottom line

Miami-Dade is two markets: houses are scarce and still gaining value, while condos give buyers a year of supply and real negotiating power. If you're moving here to live, the December 31 residency cutoff for the bigger homestead exemption is worth a call to your CPA now. And with daily rates above 7.3%, lock with a real quote, not the weekly headline.
