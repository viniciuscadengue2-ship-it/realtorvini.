Insurance

1. More than 824,300 Florida homeowners are getting lower insurance rates, and 450,000 more are next

Florida's insurance regulator and Chief Financial Officer said more than 824,300 homeowners would see rate decreases on new policies or renewals in October, with cuts of up to 12.1%. They also said another 450,000 Floridians will get decreases from pending filings that take effect in November, December and January. Among the filings already in effect, American Integrity cut rates 0.3% for about 184,000 customers and 12.3% for about 8,900, and Florida Family Home filed a decrease for about 20,700.

According to the release, 48 companies have filed for rate decreases since January 2024 and 53 have asked for no change. Insurance Commissioner Mike Yaworsky said the state is "not taking the foot off the gas." The Miami Times, republishing Florida Politics, reported the same figures on November 3.

My take: A statewide average doesn't tell you what your own renewal will look like, so read it line by line when it arrives. If your premium didn't go down, ask your agent to shop it with other carriers, and make sure your wind mitigation inspection is current, because those credits can be worth more than any rate filing. Buyers: get insurance quotes during your inspection period, not after. And if you're with Citizens, remember the flood insurance requirement I wrote about yesterday.

Source: Florida CFO / Office of Insurance Regulation · The Miami Times (Florida Politics)

Luxury

2. Palm Island lots list for $185.5 million as new South Florida trophy listings pile up

Three bayfront lots at 190, 198 and 210 Palm Avenue on Palm Island in Miami Beach are back on the market asking $185.5 million, up from their 2023 asking price, The Real Deal reported. The assemblage covers more than 2 acres with 300 feet of Biscayne Bay frontage, and comes with plans for a 30,000-square-foot, three-story mansion. The priciest recent sale on the island was 73 Palm Avenue, which sold for $60.9 million in September.

In Coral Gables, a nearly 15,000-square-foot house under construction at 165 Solano Prado is asking $75 million; the property last traded in 2024 for $17.5 million, and the listing says it could be finished in fall 2027. In Palm Beach, a 1.4-acre oceanfront estate at 1409 South Ocean Boulevard also asks $75 million. Together, the three new listings total $335.5 million. The state's most expensive listing remains 485 West Matheson Drive in Key Biscayne at $237 million.

My take: These prices show where the money is in South Florida right now: land on the water, in established neighborhoods, that can't be replicated. Asking prices at this level are opening bids, and final sale prices often come in lower. For homeowners in waterfront neighborhoods like Coral Gables, Key Biscayne or the Miami Beach islands, these listings help support your own value. If you're curious what your home would sell for today, ask me for a free home value report.

Source: The Real Deal

New Construction

3. A $97.9 million permit for 728 rentals in North Miami Beach tops the week's building permits

PPG Development, led by Ari Pearl, pulled a $97.9 million building permit for the first phase of Palm Aire, a 728-unit apartment project at 1750 Northeast 167th Street in North Miami Beach, according to The Real Deal's weekly roundup of permits issued November 3 to 11. This phase covers an eight-story and a six-story apartment building, retail and parking garages. In September, PPG and partners lined up $235 million in construction financing.

Other permits included an $18.7 million superstructure permit for 14 ROC, Russell Galbut's 32-story, 283-unit condo at 125 Northeast 14th Street in downtown Miami that allows short-term rentals; $7.1 million in permits for a 60-unit affordable building in Goulds; and a permit at Pagani Residences, the 28-story, 70-unit tower in North Bay Village where prices start at $2.4 million.

My take: Even with condo resales slow, developers are still building, and much of it is rentals. More apartments in North Miami Beach and the suburbs give renters more choices and can slow rent growth, which matters if you own a rental condo nearby. If you're buying preconstruction, a superstructure permit like the one at 14 ROC is a real milestone. Before you sign, read the rental rules, the deposit schedule and the expected delivery date.

Source: The Real Deal (top permits)

Week Ahead

4. Miami-Dade's October sales report is due Monday, with the 30-year rate at 7.11%

MIAMI Realtors' release schedule lists October existing-home sales for November 16, so Monday should bring the first full look at how Miami-Dade's market did in October, including prices, sales and inventory for single-family homes and condos.

On rates, Freddie Mac's latest weekly survey, released November 12, put the 30-year fixed average at 7.11%, down from 7.18% the week before, and the 15-year at 6.45%, down from 6.57%. A year ago they were 6.24% and 5.49%. The next weekly reading comes out Thursday.

My take: At 7.11%, principal and interest on a $500,000 30-year loan is about $3,364 a month, before taxes, insurance and HOA fees. When Monday's numbers come out, I'll break down what they mean for buyers and sellers here. If you want them for your own zip code and property type, send me a WhatsApp and I'll pull them for you.

Source: MIAMI Realtors release schedule · Freddie Mac PMMS · Freddie Mac release (GlobeNewswire)

Miami real estate questions people are asking

Are home insurance rates going down in Florida?

For many homeowners, yes. Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation and CFO said more than 824,300 homeowners would get rate decreases of up to 12.1% on new policies or renewals in October 2026, with another 450,000 expected from November through January. Your own renewal depends on your insurer, home and coverage.

What are mortgage rates today in Miami?

Freddie Mac's national weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.11% on November 12, 2026, and 6.45% for a 15-year fixed. Miami rates track national averages; your rate depends on credit, down payment and loan type.

What is the most expensive home for sale in Florida?

As of mid-November 2026, The Real Deal reported that 485 West Matheson Drive in Key Biscayne, asking $237 million, is the most expensive listing in the state. A three-lot assemblage on Palm Island in Miami Beach is asking $185.5 million.

Bottom line

Insurance relief is reaching hundreds of thousands of Florida homeowners, so check your renewal and shop it if it didn't drop. The top of the market keeps setting new asking-price marks, developers are still breaking ground, and Monday's October report will tell us more about everyone else.
