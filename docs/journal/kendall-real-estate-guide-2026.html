Kendall in 2026: A Buyer's Guide to Miami's Space-and-Value Suburb

Kendall gives Miami buyers more space for the money, but the name covers a lot of ground. Here is how to choose the right corner of it.

By Vinicius Cadengue, Riley Smith Group at Compass
Nov. 24, 2026 · 4 min read

“We want a yard, a commute we can live with and a pool the kids will actually use. Is that still possible in Miami?”

When a family asks me that, the conversation usually ends up in Kendall. Not because it’s trendy. Nobody has ever called Kendall trendy, and I say that with love. It’s because so much of Miami’s everyday life happens there: school runs, Saturday soccer, a quick Sunday trip to Dadeland that somehow takes four hours.

Kendall matters because it answers a question the rest of the city often can’t: how do you get more house and more space without leaving Miami-Dade? Relocating families, buyers from Brazil and the rest of Latin America who want a calmer home base, and investors looking at long-term rentals should all give it a real look. Here is how I walk clients through it.

Which Kendall do you mean?

First, a little geography, because it confuses almost everyone. Kendall is not a city. It is an unincorporated area and census-designated place in Miami-Dade County, and the census area alone counted 80,241 residents in 2020. But when people say “Kendall,” they usually mean something much bigger: a wide stretch of southwest Miami-Dade that reaches west toward the Turnpike and takes in neighbors like Kendale Lakes, The Crossings and Three Lakes, plus the area most locals simply call West Kendall.

The east side, near U.S. 1 and Dadeland Mall, feels closer to the city. That’s where you find more condos, apartments and townhomes, close to the mall and the Metrorail. Head west and the communities get more planned, the yards get bigger and the drive into town gets longer. Same name, very different mornings. So when someone tells me they want “a house in Kendall,” my first question is always which one.

The name comes from Henry John Broughton Kendall, a director of the Florida Land and Mortgage Company, which bought much of the land from the State of Florida in 1883. More than a century later, people still come to Kendall for the same thing: land.

“Same name, very different mornings.”

What you can buy, and what to check

Most buyers I bring to Kendall are looking at single-family homes, many with yards and pools. There are also plenty of townhomes, villas and condos, which is a good thing if you want the neighborhood without the lawn. If you’re torn between the two, my guide on condo vs. single-family homes in Miami lays out the trade-offs.

Many neighborhoods sit inside planned communities with homeowners associations, so I read the HOA rules with buyers before anyone falls in love with a kitchen. Fences, paint colors, parking, rentals, even whether you can leave a work truck in the driveway. Better to know on day one than after closing.

Then there’s the house itself. Kendall and the surrounding South Dade area were severely damaged by Hurricane Andrew in August 1992. That history is one more reason I look hard at roof age, window and door protection and when the big systems were last updated. Those details affect your safety, and they affect what insurance will cost. I ask buyers to get an insurance quote before we write an offer, not after. And check the flood zone on FEMA’s map for the exact address, because in South Florida it can change from one block to the next.

Getting around

The honest answer about Kendall commutes is that it depends on where you live and when you leave. U.S. 1 and Kendall Drive get crowded at rush hour, and the farther west you go, the more your day depends on the Turnpike and the Don Shula Expressway.

East Kendall has a real advantage here. Metrorail stops at Dadeland North and Dadeland South, and Dadeland South is the southern end of the line, with connections to the South Dade TransitWay buses. The Underline, the county’s 10-mile linear park beneath the Metrorail tracks, runs from the Miami River in Brickell down to the Dadeland South station. If you want the option of taking the train to Brickell, or a long weekend bike ride, living near Dadeland changes the math.

My advice is simple. Before you buy, do the drive. Leave from the actual street at 7:45 on a weekday and see where you are at 8:30. I’ve watched that one test settle more debates than any spreadsheet.

Who Kendall fits best

Families who want space are the obvious match. So are buyers who want to stay in Miami-Dade but find Coral Gables, Pinecrest or Coconut Grove out of reach. Clients often ask me why they should look west of U.S. 1, and the usual answer is more square footage for the budget while staying within reach of the city. Daily life is convenient, too. Dadeland Mall is right there, and Miami Dade College’s Kendall Campus has served the area since 1967.

For investors, Kendall is mostly a long-term rental story. Family homes and townhomes in established areas appeal to tenants who plan to stay a while. Just confirm the HOA’s leasing rules first, since some communities limit rentals, and run the full numbers, including insurance, before you get attached. My cost calculator is a good place to start.

For my international clients, Kendall is often the “real life” option next to a beachfront condo. It’s where you live, not where you vacation. If that sounds like what you’re after, my Kendall neighborhood page has the basics, and I’m happy to send you homes that fit your budget.

Questions readers ask

Is Kendall its own city?No. Kendall is an unincorporated area and census-designated place in Miami-Dade County, so it is not a separate municipality. Locals also use the name loosely for a much larger part of southwest Miami-Dade, including West Kendall.

Can I get to Brickell from Kendall without a car?From the Dadeland area, yes. Metrorail stops at Dadeland North and Dadeland South, and the Underline linear park runs beneath the tracks from Dadeland South to the Miami River in Brickell. Farther west, most residents rely on a car.

Is Kendall a good place to buy a rental property?It can be, especially for long-term rentals in family homes and townhomes. Check the HOA's leasing rules and get an insurance quote before you make an offer, since both can change your numbers.
