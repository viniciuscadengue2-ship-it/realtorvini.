Why the Holidays Are a Smart Time to Buy in Miami

While most buyers pause for December, the ones who keep looking often find less competition, sellers ready to deal and a head start on the new year.

By Vinicius Cadengue, Riley Smith Group at Compass
Nov. 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Every November, a client tells me the same thing: “Let’s wait until after the holidays.”

I understand the instinct. December in Miami is a blur of family dinners, airport runs and a calendar that fills itself. Buying a home feels like something that belongs to a calmer month.

But for a buyer who is ready, the stretch between Thanksgiving and New Year’s can be the most productive few weeks of the year. The reason is simple: when most people stop looking, the ones who keep going have the market a little more to themselves.

Fewer people at the door

Most buyers take December off. Open houses get quieter, and multiple-offer situations become less common. That changes the conversation. With fewer competing offers, there is more room to talk about price, and just as often about everything around it: closing costs, repairs, appliances, the move-in date.

In the spring, a buyer might be one of five offers on a home. In December, they are often the only one the seller has seen all week.

“Nobody lists a home three days before Christmas for fun.”

Sellers with a reason

The sellers who list this time of year usually have one. It might be a job relocation, a new home already under contract, an estate to settle or a plan to close before the year ends.

Motivated sellers tend to respond faster and are more open to reasonable terms. When I represent buyers in December, one of my first questions to the listing agent is what matters most to the seller. Often the answer is speed and certainty, not the last dollar.

A trip that does double duty

For many of my Brazilian and Latin American clients, December and January are when the family is already in Miami, since Brazil’s school summer break falls in this window. The trip planned for the holidays can also be the trip that gets a purchase done.

In a single visit, a buyer can tour a short list in person instead of by video, sit down with a lender who works with foreign nationals and talk with a cross-border CPA about whether to buy in a personal name, through an LLC or another way. It is also a chance to see a neighborhood at the hours that matter: school drop-off, rush hour, a Saturday night.

Buyers who do their homework before they fly can go home with a contract, not just photos on their phone. (If you’re buying from abroad, my international buyer guide covers the basics.)

The catch, and how to plan around it

There is one real trade-off. Lenders, title companies, inspectors and appraisers take time off, too. A purchase can close in December, but the timeline needs cushion. I book inspections the day an offer is accepted, plan for slower responses between Dec. 24 and Jan. 1, and ask international buyers to start their wires early, since they can take extra days to clear. It is also worth asking a CPA whether closing before or after Dec. 31 changes anything on this year’s taxes.

Then January arrives. New listings return, and so do the buyers who spent the holidays making resolutions. The ones who used December well are already negotiating while everyone else is just starting to search.

Questions readers ask

Are home prices lower in December?Not automatically. The advantage is less competition and more room to negotiate, not a holiday price tag. The savings often show up in credits and terms.

Can a foreign national buy during a short visit?Yes. Much of the process, including signing, can be done remotely. Having the lender, title company and agent lined up before you arrive is what makes a short trip productive.

Should I wait for rates to drop?If the right home appears, you can buy now and look at refinancing later if rates fall. Waiting for the perfect rate often means competing with many more buyers when it arrives. You can run your own numbers with my cost calculator.
